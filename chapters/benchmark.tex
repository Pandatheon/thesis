\chapter{A benchmark for indefinite OCP QPs} % Outline text
\label{Chapter::Benchmark}
The goal of this chapter is to provide test problems for evaluating the performance of the proposed Riccati-based primal regularizations
on the subproblems of \gls{SQP}.
To this end, we construct a benchmark of indefinite \gls{OCP} \glspl{QP}
that arise in the \gls{SQP} iterations of \gls{NMPC} problems.
First, in Section \ref{sec:benchmark_construction},
we will introduce the construction of the \gls{QP} benchmark, 
including the formulation of \gls{QP} subproblems in the benchmark, an overview of \gls{OCP} test problems 
and the pipeline for collecting indefinite \glspl{QP}.
Then we will analyze the collected problem instances to gain a better understanding of characteristics
regarding definiteness in the indefinite \gls{OCP} \gls{QP} benchmark in Section \ref{sec:benchmark_analysis}.

\section{Benchmark construction}
\label{sec:benchmark_construction}
This section describes the construction of the indefinite \gls{QP} benchmark.
We first formulate the \gls{QP} subproblems from the \gls{SQP} iterations of the \glspl{OCP},
then introduce the eight \gls{OCP} test problems,
formulated as fixed-time regulator problems with quadratic stage costs and box constraints on the controls,
and finally the collection pipeline.

\subsection{Formulation of QP subproblems from OCPs}
\label{subsec:formulation}
We will start from the formulation of the nonlinear \glspl{OCP} 
to obtain the resulting \gls{QP} subproblems that arise in the \gls{SQP} iterations.
In the benchmark, all \glspl{OCP} share the same structure: 
a fixed-horizon, direct multiple-shooting \gls{OCP} 
with a quadratic stage cost that penalizes deviations from given state and control references.
The mathematical formulation of the \glspl{OCP} is given as follows:
\begin{mini}[2]
    {x, u}
    {\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \frac{1}{2}
        \begin{bmatrix} x_k-x_k^{\mathrm{ref}} \\ u_k-u_k^{\mathrm{ref}} \end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}
        \begin{bmatrix} \mathcal{Q}_k & \mathcal{S}_k^{\top} \\ \mathcal{S}_k & \mathcal{R}_k \end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix} x_k-x_k^{\mathrm{ref}} \\ u_k-u_k^{\mathrm{ref}} \end{bmatrix}}
    {\label{eq:ocp}}
    {}
    \breakObjective{+ \tfrac12\|x_N-x_N^{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{\mathcal{Q}_N}^2}
    \addConstraint{x_0}{= \bar{x}_0}
    \addConstraint{x_{k+1}}{= f_k(x_k,u_k),\quad}{k = 0, \ldots, N-1}
    \addConstraint{\underline{g}}{\le \mathcal{C}_k x_k + \mathcal{D}_k u_k \le \bar{g},}{k = 0, \ldots, N-1}
    \addConstraint{\underline{h}}{\le h_k(x_k,u_k) \le \bar{h},}{k = 0, \ldots, N-1}
    \addConstraint{\underline{g}_N}{\le \mathcal{C}_N x_N \le \bar{g}_N,}
    \addConstraint{\underline{h}_N}{\le h_N(x_N) \le \bar{h}_N,}
\end{mini}
where $x_{k+1}=f_k(x_k,u_k)$ is one integration step of the continuous dynamics over $[t_k,t_{k+1}]$.
The given initial state is $\bar{x}_0$, $\mathcal{Q}_k,\mathcal{R}_k,\mathcal{Q}_N\succeq 0$ are weighting matrices, 
$\mathcal{S}_k$ is the coupling cost matrix, and $x_k^{\mathrm{ref}},u_k^{\mathrm{ref}}$ are the reference points. 
The term $\mathcal{C}_k x_k + \mathcal{D}_k u_k$ includes the affine and box path constraints,
$\underline{g}_N \le \mathcal{C}_N x_N \le \bar{g}_N$ are the affine terminal constraints, and
$h_k(\cdot, \cdot)$ and $h_N(\cdot)$ collect the nonlinear path and terminal constraints, respectively.

When using the exact Hessian of the Lagrangian in the \gls{SQP} iterations, the resulting Hessian of the \gls{QP} subproblem at stage $k = 0, \dots, N-1$ reads
\begin{align}
    H_k &= \nabla^2 \mathcal{L}(x, u, \pi, \lambda)  \notag \\
        &= \begin{bmatrix} \mathcal{Q}_k & \mathcal{S}_k^{\top} \\ \mathcal{S}_k & \mathcal{R}_k \end{bmatrix} + \sum_{j=1}^{n_x} (\pi_k)_j \nabla^2 [f_k]_j(x_k,u_k) + \sum_{j=1}^{n_h} (\lambda_k)_j \nabla^2 [h_k]_j(x_k,u_k),
\end{align}
where $n_h$ is the number of nonlinear path constraints,
and $\pi_k$ and $\lambda_k$ are the Lagrange multipliers associated with the dynamics and nonlinear constraints, respectively.

The definiteness of $H_k$ is thus determined by two parts,
a constant cost block  $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathcal{Q}_k & \mathcal{S}_k^{\top}\\ \mathcal{S}_k & \mathcal{R}_k\end{smallmatrix}\right]$
and an iterate-dependent cost from the two curvature terms.
In the latter, each component Hessian $\nabla^2[f_k]_j$ and $\nabla^2[h_k]_j$ is symmetric but carries no definiteness guarantee, 
and the associated multipliers $(\pi_k)_j$ and $(\lambda_k)_j$ may take arbitrary sign and magnitude during the \gls{SQP} iterations.

The full Hessian blocks $H_k$ are thus general symmetric and typically indefinite matrices, in particular when the multipliers are large.
Consequently, the exact-Hessian \gls{QP} subproblem is in general nonconvex.

As for $H_N$, the dynamics and the affine terminal constraints add no curvature in $x_N$,
but the nonlinear terminal constraints do, so
\begin{equation*}
    H_N = \mathcal{Q}_N + \sum_{j=1}^{n_{h_N}} (\lambda_N)_j \nabla^2 [h_N]_j(x_N),
\end{equation*}
where $n_{h_N}$ is the number of nonlinear terminal constraints and $\lambda_N$ are their multipliers. Like $H_k$, $H_N$ is thus iterate-dependent and possibly indefinite.

Collecting the stage Hessians $H_k$ and Jacobians 
and linearizing the constraints of \eqref{eq:ocp} at the current \gls{SQP} iterate 
yields a structured \gls{QP}. The subproblem takes the form of
\begin{mini}[2]
    {\Delta x, \Delta u}
    {\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left( \frac{1}{2}
        \begin{bmatrix} \Delta x_k \\ \Delta u_k \end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}
        \begin{bmatrix} Q_k & S_k^\top \\ S_k & R_k \end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix} \Delta x_k \\ \Delta u_k \end{bmatrix}
        + \begin{bmatrix} q_k \\ r_k \end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}
        \begin{bmatrix} \Delta x_k \\ \Delta u_k \end{bmatrix} \right)}
    {}
    {}
    \breakObjective{+ \tfrac{1}{2}\, \Delta x_N^\top Q_N\, \Delta x_N + q_N^\top \Delta x_N}
    \addConstraint{\Delta x_0}{= \Delta \bar{x}_0}
    \addConstraint{\Delta x_{k+1}}{= A_k \Delta x_k + B_k \Delta u_k + c_k,\quad}{k = 0, \ldots, N-1}
    \addConstraint{\begin{bmatrix} \underline{u}_k \\ \underline{x}_k \\ \underline{d}_k \end{bmatrix}}
        {\le \begin{bmatrix} J_k^{b,u} & 0 \\ 0 & J_k^{b,x} \\ D_k & C_k \end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix} \Delta u_k \\ \Delta x_k \end{bmatrix}
        \le \begin{bmatrix} \overline{u}_k \\ \overline{x}_k \\ \overline{d}_k \end{bmatrix},}
        {k = 0, \ldots, N}
\end{mini}
where, at each stage $k$, $\Delta x_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x}$ and $\Delta u_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n_u}$
denote the state and control deviation variables at stage $k$.
Note that $\Delta x_k$ and $\Delta u_k$ here denote the \gls{SQP} step,
which the \gls{QP} solver treats as its decision variables.

All remaining quantities are obtained by linearizing the \gls{OCP} at the
current \gls{SQP} iterate $(x_k,u_k)$ with multipliers $(\pi_k,\lambda_k)$.

In the objective, $Q_k$, $R_k$ and $S_k$ are the state, control and coupling blocks of the exact Lagrangian Hessian $H_k$,
and the stage-cost gradients are
$q_k=\mathcal{Q}_k(x_k-x_k^{\mathrm{ref}})+\mathcal{S}_k^{\top}(u_k-u_k^{\mathrm{ref}})$ and
$r_k=\mathcal{R}_k(u_k-u_k^{\mathrm{ref}})+\mathcal{S}_k(x_k-x_k^{\mathrm{ref}})$,
with $Q_N$ and $q_N$ as the terminal Hessian and gradient.

In the equality constraints, $A_k=\nabla_{x_k} f_k$ and $B_k=\nabla_{u_k} f_k$ are the Jacobians of the discretized dynamics,
$c_k=f_k(x_k,u_k)-x_{k+1}$ is the dynamics residual, which vanishes at a dynamically feasible iterate,
and $\Delta\bar{x}_0=\bar{x}_0-x_0$ is the residual of the initial state constraint $x_0=\bar{x}_0$.

In the inequality constraints, $J_k^{b,u}$ and $J_k^{b,x}$ are selection matrices that pick out the bounded controls and states,
with bounds $\underline{u}_k,\overline{u}_k$ and $\underline{x}_k,\overline{x}_k$ taken 
relative to the current iterate, like $\underline{d}_k,\overline{d}_k$ below.
The matrices $C_k=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathcal{C}_k\\ \nabla_{x_k} h_k\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ and
$D_k=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathcal{D}_k\\ \nabla_{u_k} h_k\end{smallmatrix}\right]$
stack the Jacobians of the affine and nonlinear path constraints, with the bounds
\begin{equation*}
    \underline{d}_k=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{g}-\mathcal{C}_k x_k-\mathcal{D}_k u_k\\ \underline{h}-h_k(x_k,u_k)\end{bmatrix}, \qquad
    \overline{d}_k=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{g}-\mathcal{C}_k x_k-\mathcal{D}_k u_k\\ \bar{h}-h_k(x_k,u_k)\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
At the terminal stage $k=N$, no control is present, so $J_N^{b,u}$ and $D_N$ are empty,
and $C_N$, $\underline{d}_N$ and $\overline{d}_N$ are formed in the same way from $\mathcal{C}_N x_N$ and $h_N(x_N)$
with the bounds $\underline{g}_N,\bar{g}_N$ and $\underline{h}_N,\bar{h}_N$.

\subsection{Benchmark OCPs}
\label{subsec:benchmark_ocps}
In this subsection, we introduce the eight \glspl{OCP} used to construct the benchmark. 
Each \gls{OCP} is based on a dynamic system provided in~\cite{Kiessling2026} 
and formulated as a setpoint regulation problem with problem-specific modifications,
which can be uniformly described with \eqref{eq:ocp}.

The eight \glspl{OCP} are named and characterized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pendulum:} Swing-up of an inverted pendulum on a cart under an input bound and a mixed state--input constraint.
    \item \textbf{Acrobot:} Adapted from \cite{spong1995swing}.
    An underactuated two-link pendulum moved between two prescribed configurations.
    \item \textbf{Dubins car:} Adapted from \cite{dubins1957curves}.
    Parallel parking of a kinematic unicycle in a narrow corridor.
    \item \textbf{Furuta:} Adapted from \cite{furuta1992swing}.
    Swing-up of a rotary inverted pendulum under tight torque limits.
    \item \textbf{Flying robot:} Adapted from \cite{betts2010practical}.
    A planar free-flying robot steered by four nonnegative thrusters.
    \item \textbf{Robot arm:} Adapted from \cite{betts2010practical}.
    A three-degree-of-freedom arm moved to a new set-point, with nonlinear input coupling.
    \item \textbf{Crane:} Adapted from \cite{betts2010practical}.
    An overhead crane moving a load between two rest positions.
    \item \textbf{Moonlander:} Adapted from \cite{meditch1964optimal}.
    Soft landing of a planar lunar lander with two thrusters.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Collection pipeline}
Based on the eight \glspl{OCP}, 
we can construct a benchmark of indefinite \glspl{QP} arising from \gls{SQP}-based \gls{NMPC}. 
Specifically, we establish a collection pipeline that extracts the \gls{QP} subproblems directly from intermediate \gls{SQP} iterations.
The collection pipeline consists of two steps: \emph{generate} and \emph{collect}, and can be summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generate:} In this step, we generate iterates by solving the \gls{OCP} with \gls{SQP} 
    and obtain iterates from early to late \gls{SQP} iterations.
    To keep the \gls{SQP} iterations from failing on indefinite exact-Hessian \glspl{QP}, 
    we use \gls{SQP}-level regularization or the Gauss--Newton Hessian approximation in \texttt{acados}.

    \item \textbf{Collect:} In this step, 
    we utilize the previously generated iterates as initial guesses for linearization within the solver. 
    We can selectively collect indefinite \glspl{QP} that exhibit an indefinite reduced Hessian.
\end{enumerate}

In the collect step, HPIPM uses the classical Riccati recursion (\texttt{ric\_alg = 0})~\cite{frisonAlgorithmsMethodsHighPerformance},
which requires only the reduced Hessian to be positive definite.
A failure of the recursion thus indicates an indefinite reduced Hessian, which \texttt{acados} reports as status \texttt{4} (infeasible \gls{QP}),
and only the \glspl{QP} with this status are collected.

For each problem, we keep only the \glspl{QP} that the reference solver IPOPT~\cite{wachter2006implementation} solves successfully,
and limit the number of near-identical \glspl{QP}, identified by comparing their solutions,
so that the number of instances per problem stays within a comparable range and the collected instances remain meaningful.
In the end, we obtain a total of $175$ indefinite \glspl{QP} from the eight \glspl{OCP}.
The per-problem counts are reported in Table~\ref{tab:collected_qps} and range from $15$ to $29$.
These \glspl{QP} are stored in JSON format and available at \url{https://github.com/Pandatheon/Primal-dual_IPM.git}.

\section{Benchmark analysis}
\label{sec:benchmark_analysis}
In this section,
we will analyze the collected instances regarding the definiteness in the benchmark.
The distribution of indefiniteness is analyzed in Section \ref{subsec:distribution},
which describes the magnitude and coverage of the indefinite reduced Hessians in the collected \glspl{QP}.
The source of indefiniteness is analyzed in Section \ref{subsec:source}, 
which further illustrates the source of indefiniteness in the collected \glspl{QP} 
by analyzing the eigenvalues of the reduced Hessian blocks at each stage.

\subsection{Distribution of indefiniteness}
\label{subsec:distribution}
We first characterize the collected \glspl{QP} along two axes:
the \emph{depth} of indefiniteness, 
measured by the magnitude of the most negative eigenvalue $|\nu_{\min}|$ of the reduced Hessian,
and the \emph{width}, measured by the fraction of reduced Hessian blocks that are indefinite within a \gls{QP}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{images/benchmark/indefiniteness_coverage_scatter.pdf}
    \caption{Depth ($|\nu_{\min}|$) versus width (indefinite-stage rate) of the collected QPs,
    colored by OCP. Grey lines mark the medians.}
    \label{fig:indefinite_qp_scatter}
\end{figure}

As seen from Figure~\ref{fig:indefinite_qp_scatter}, 
the depth of the collected \glspl{QP} ranges from near zero to very large values, and the width from a few stages to the entire horizon.
But wide and deep indefiniteness seldom occur together.
Problems that are indefinite in only a small fraction of their stages, 
such as \textsl{Crane}, \textsl{Flying robot} and \textsl{Furuta}, can still reach deep negative curvature.
Conversely, \textsl{Dubins car} and \textsl{Robot arm} show only moderate depth with wide indefinite-stage rates.
\textsl{Moonlander} and \textsl{Acrobot} lie in between, with intermediate indefinite-stage rates.
\textsl{Moonlander} stays within a narrow depth band, whereas \textsl{Acrobot} spans almost the full depth range of the benchmark.
\textsl{Pendulum} contributes \glspl{QP} with very shallow negative curvature at intermediate rates.
The eight \glspl{OCP} thus cover narrow-but-deep, intermediate and wide-but-moderate indefiniteness.

\subsection{Source of indefiniteness}
\label{subsec:source}
Recall that the reduced Hessian block, $\tilde{M}_k = \tilde{R}_k + B_k^\top P_{k+1}B_k$, at stage $k$, 
is assembled from two parts, the control cost block $\tilde{R}_k$ and the cost-to-go block $P_{k+1}$.

To investigate where the indefiniteness originates,
we classify every indefinite stage by which of $R_k$ and $P_{k+1}$ has a negative eigenvalue:
\emph{R only} ($\tilde{R}_k \not\succeq 0$, $P_{k+1} \succeq 0$),
\emph{P only} ($P_{k+1} \not\succeq 0$, $R_k \succeq 0$),
and \emph{both} ($\tilde{R}_k, P_{k+1} \not\succeq 0$).
Since $\tilde{R}_k, P_{k+1} \succeq 0$ would imply $M_k \succeq 0$, these three cases cover all indefinite stages.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{images/benchmark/stage_indefiniteness_signature.pdf}
    \caption{Classification of the indefinite stages of each OCP into \emph{R only}, \emph{both} and \emph{P only}
    ($n$ is the number of collected indefinite stages).}
    \label{fig:indefinite_source_signature}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:indefinite_source_signature} shows this classification for each \gls{OCP}.
In every \gls{OCP}, most indefinite stages involve $P$, either alone or together with $R$,
and six of the eight \glspl{OCP} are dominated by a single category,
with only \textsl{Acrobot} and \textsl{Crane} mixed.

The \emph{both} case dominates \textsl{Moonlander}, \textsl{Robot arm}, \textsl{Dubins car} and \textsl{Furuta}.
The \emph{P only} case dominates \textsl{Flying robot} and \textsl{Pendulum},
and is also the largest category in the two mixed problems, \textsl{Crane} and \textsl{Acrobot}, ahead of \emph{both}.
The \emph{R only} case appears in four problems and is substantial only for \textsl{Dubins car},
while marginal in \textsl{Robot arm}, \textsl{Acrobot} and \textsl{Crane}.

Among all, \textsl{Flying robot} is the clearest case: all of its indefinite stages are \emph{P only}, 
with $\tilde{R}_k \succ 0$ throughout.
Its negative curvature thus enters the reduced Hessian only through the cost-to-go block, at isolated stages.

\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{Chapter::Abstract}
{

    In \gls{NMPC}, \gls{SQP} with the exact Hessian of the Lagrangian
    produces \gls{QP} subproblems of which the full Hessian can be indefinite.
    Structure-exploiting \gls{QP} solvers embedded in the \gls{SQP} solver often require a positive definite full Hessian,
    so the full Hessian is regularized at the \gls{SQP} level before it is passed to the solver,
    which in general changes the solution of each \gls{QP} and also the feedback in \gls{SQP} with real-time iteration.
    In contrast to SQP-level regularizations,
    regularizing at the \gls{QP} level can introduce two advantages.
    First, this lets the solver exploit the curvature from inequality constraints without extra estimation, 
    and, second, only return the solution of the original QP.

    This thesis investigates primal regularizations inside interior-point solvers 
    for indefinite \glspl{QP} with \gls{OCP} structure, with a focus on the proposed Riccati-based regularization,
    which regularizes only at the stages where the Cholesky factorization fails during Riccati recursion.
    This thesis shows with a general case that the minimum perturbation strategy can make the Riccati-based regularization fail, 
    and further derives an improved strategy from the case to avoid such failure.
    It is proven that the Riccati-based regularization becomes inactive near the solution.

    For evaluation, this thesis builds a benchmark of $175$ indefinite \gls{OCP} \glspl{QP} from the \gls{SQP} iterations.
    With the regularizations implemented in a prototype solver that reproduces the iterations of HPIPM on convex \glspl{QP},
    this thesis compares the Riccati-based regularization with full Hessian regularization and inertia correction 
    in terms of robustness, efficiency and estimated factorization cost.

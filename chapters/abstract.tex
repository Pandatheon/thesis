\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{Chapter::Abstract}
{

    In \gls{NMPC}, \gls{SQP} with the exact Hessian of the Lagrangian
    produces \gls{QP} subproblems of which the full Hessian can be indefinite.
    Structure-exploiting \gls{QP} solvers embedded in the \gls{SQP} solver often require a \gls{PD} full Hessian for carrying out iteration,
    so the full Hessian is regularized at the \gls{SQP} level before it is passed to the solver,
    which in general changes the solution of each \gls{QP} and thus, in real-time iteration, directly changes the feedback.

    This thesis investigates the regularizations that handle the indefiniteness inside interior-point solvers.
    By regularizing at the \gls{QP} level,
    the solver can take advantage of the barrier term in the \gls{IPM} that adds curvature on the constraints as they become active, 
    without extra estimation of the active set,
    and the solver still returns the solution of the original \gls{QP} when the \gls{QP} is indefinite.

    On the algorithmic side,
    we propose a primal regularization inside the Riccati recursion, namely Riccati-based regularization,
    which regularizes only at the stages where the Cholesky factorization fails.
    We show that the minimum perturbation strategy can make the regularization fail,
    derive an improved strategy, and prove that the regularization becomes inactive near the solution.
    On the experimental side,
    for evaluation, we build a benchmark of $175$ indefinite \gls{OCP} \glspl{QP}, collected from the \gls{SQP} iterations of eight \glspl{OCP}.
    We implement the methods in a prototype solver that reproduces the iterations of HPIPM on convex \glspl{QP},
    and compare them with full Hessian regularization and inertia correction in terms of robustness and estimated factorization cost.

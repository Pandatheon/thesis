\chapter{Conclusion and outlook} % Overview text
\label{Chapter::Conclusion}

\paragraph{Conclusion}
This thesis investigated the regularizations of indefinite \gls{QP} subproblems
from exact-Hessian \gls{SQP} for \gls{NMPC} inside the interior-point solver,
where the barrier curvature is already included and only the reduced Hessian needs to be positive definite.
Since the Riccati backward pass is a block $\mathrm{LD}\mathrm{L}^\top$ factorization of the reduced Hessian,
this thesis proposes Riccati-based regularizations, which regularize the reduced Hessian stage-wise
wherever the Cholesky factorization of a reduced Hessian block $\tilde{M}_k$ fails while keeping the computational cost linear in the horizon length.

This thesis showed that the minimum perturbation strategy applied to a reduced Hessian block $\tilde{M}_k$
can make the preceding stages indefinite and ill-conditioned.
The improved strategy therefore uses a non-minimal correction and also regularizes the cost-to-go matrix.
It is also proved that the regularization becomes inactive close to the solution, so the local convergence rate of the \gls{IPM} is retained.

With Mehrotra's probing, \textsf{PM\_MIRROR} and \textsf{PM\_GMW} solved all $170$ instances,
\textsf{PM\_GMW} at a lower factorization cost than \textsf{PM\_MIRROR},
against $21$ to $48$ unsolved for full Hessian regularization and $9$ for inertia correction.
The full Hessian methods failed on almost every \textsl{Flying robot} instance,
where only the cost-to-go matrix is indefinite, and only at a few stages.
Under the conditional predictor--corrector, however, the ranking reverses,
and \textsf{PM\_MIRROR} loses far more instances than \textsf{INERTIA\_LM}.

\paragraph{Outlook}
Several questions remain open regarding the benchmark, the regularizations and the solver.

The considered benchmark is limited to small-scale \glspl{QP} with \gls{OCP} structure from regulator problems.
First, scaling up the benchmark to larger state and control dimensions
would show whether \textsf{PM\_MIRROR} and \textsf{PM\_GMW} remain robust beyond small-scale \glspl{QP}
and how their factorization cost could grow.
Second, in time-optimal and economic MPC,
the cost provides little positive curvature, so the indefiniteness is likely to differ from that in this benchmark and to be harder to handle.
Whether the ranking of the methods carries over remains to be tested.

Further directions are worth trying for regularizations.
First, the regularization magnitude is set by a fixed eigenvalue floor $\epsilon$.
Adapting the magnitude, e.g.\ to the conditioning of the corrected blocks, could improve efficiency and robustness on other problems.
Second, a local Levenberg--Marquardt variant remains to be explored,
which adds a multiple of the identity to the stage block where the factorization fails 
and to a tunable number of its neighboring stage blocks instead of to every block, 
so that extending it to all stages recovers inertia correction.

The QPs were solved in isolation with a prototype solver in Python.
First, an implementation in HPIPM and \texttt{acados} is needed for a run-time comparison.
Second, since the full step from the indefinite QP solution might be too aggressive,
it remains to be investigated how the globalization mechanisms, such as a trust region and line search,
should be designed for SQP with the indefinite QP solver for good closed-loop performance.
